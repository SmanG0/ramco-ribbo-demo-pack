# Design Service and 3D Visualisation

> **Source:** kitchensandbeyond.co.ke, company LinkedIn.
> `[VERIFY]` Whether design and 3D visualisation are chargeable, at what point in the process they happen, typical turnaround, and what the client receives — all require confirmation before go-live.

## What is offered

Kitchens & Beyond provides **in-house interior design services** and **3D interior design visualisation**.

The 3D visualisation is a genuine differentiator and worth leading with on renovation enquiries. A client committing significant money to a kitchen they cannot yet see is nervous, and being shown the finished space before ordering removes most of that anxiety.

## Why this matters to the bot

It gives the bot something concrete and valuable to offer that is not a price.

A client asking "how much" can be redirected toward "let's show you what it could look like" — which is a better conversation, a stronger reason to visit the showroom, and completely free of the pricing risk in `kb-budget-and-pricing.md`.

> "Rather than guess at a number, the more useful next step is usually a 3D visualisation — you'd see the actual space laid out before committing to anything. Do you have drawings or measurements?"

## What to capture for a design conversation

1. **Room dimensions**, or whether the client has drawings
2. **Whether an architect is involved**, and at what stage
3. **Property stage** — new build, gut renovation, replacement within existing layout
4. **What they want the space to do** — how they cook, who uses it, storage priorities, entertaining
5. **Style direction**, in their own words. "Clean and modern" or "warm, lots of wood" is more useful to a designer than a brand name
6. **Any fixed constraints** — existing plumbing, windows, structural walls, a worktop they already own

That fifth point is where a bot can outperform a web form. Asking someone how they actually use their kitchen, and recording the answer in their own words, gives a designer something to work with.

## Drawings and files

`[VERIFY]` Confirm the correct route for clients to submit architect's drawings and measurements, and any file size or format guidance.

**The bot should not accept drawings through the chat channel.** Architectural drawings are large, often multi-page, and lose fidelity through messaging platforms. Direct them to email or an upload route, and note on the brief that drawings are coming.

## Managing expectations

`[VERIFY]` Do not state turnaround or cost for design work until confirmed.

The bot may explain **that** design and 3D visualisation are part of the service and that a designer will walk them through it. It may not commit to how long it takes, what it costs, or how many revisions are included.

## The showroom connection

Design conversations work best in person, surrounded by the materials. Where a client engages on design, the natural next step is a showroom appointment — see `kb-showroom-visits.md`.

The bot's job is to make that connection feel obvious rather than pushy: the designer can do far more with the client in the room than over a message.
