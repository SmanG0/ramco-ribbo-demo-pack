# The Project Process

> `[VERIFY]` **Stage names, durations and responsibilities must be confirmed with Kitchens & Beyond.** The sequence below is a reasonable reconstruction for a luxury interiors fit-out, not their documented process.
> Do not state a stage name or duration to a client until verified.

## Indicative sequence

`[VERIFY]` each stage, then align the data model to the confirmed names.

1. **Enquiry** — captured, qualified, appointment booked
2. **Showroom consultation** — client meets a designer, sees materials
3. **Survey / measurement** — site measured, constraints established
4. **Design & 3D visualisation** — layout produced and reviewed
5. **Client selections** — worktops, doors, finishes, tiles, appliances, hardware
6. **Quotation & approval** — priced against the final specification
7. **Order placed** — imported items ordered from Italy and elsewhere
8. **Delivery to site**
9. **Installation**
10. **Handover & snagging**

## Where projects stall — and it is usually the client

Two stages sit on the **client's** action, not on Kitchens & Beyond's:

- **Client selections** — a scheme cannot be ordered until every finish is chosen. Tiles, worktop, door finish, handles. Clients delay these because they are hard, aesthetic, irreversible decisions
- **Quotation approval** — nothing is ordered until the quote is signed off

Because much of the range is **imported**, a delay at selection stage does not just push the project by the length of the delay. It can miss a shipping window and push it considerably further.

**This is the bot's most valuable moment.** When a client asks about their project and it is held at selections, lead with it — plainly, helpfully, and with the consequence stated.

> **Good:** "You're all set apart from the tile selection — that's the last piece before we can place the order. Because the Caesar tiles come in from Italy, getting that confirmed this week protects your March installation. Would it help to book a half hour at the showroom to settle it?"
>
> **Bad:** "Your project is delayed pending your material selections."

The first names the blocker, explains the stake, and offers a concrete way to unblock it. The second assigns blame and offers nothing.

## Lead times

`[VERIFY]` Obtain indicative lead times per category — kitchens, wardrobes, sanitaryware, tiles — distinguishing stocked items from imported-to-order, and confirm how shipping windows work.

**Do not state a lead time or an installation date.** The bot may report a project's current stage and what is outstanding. Committing to dates is the project manager's job.

## Installation

`[VERIFY]` Confirm whether installation is carried out in-house or by approved contractors, what site preparation the client is responsible for, and how installation is scheduled.

This matters because clients frequently ask what they need to have ready — plumbing, electrics, floor levels — and a wrong answer creates an expensive site problem.

## Handover and snagging

`[VERIFY]` Confirm the snagging process and how a client raises an issue after handover.

Post-handover issues should be captured with detail and handed off promptly. A client with a problem on a completed luxury installation needs a person, quickly. See `kb-warranty.md`.

## What the bot does

- Report current project stage and what is outstanding
- Lead with any blocker sitting on the client, and state the consequence
- Offer a concrete unblocking step, usually a showroom visit or a call
- Capture snagging reports with detail and escalate
- Explain the process in general terms

## What the bot does not do

- Commit to installation or delivery dates
- Confirm a lead time
- Vary a specification or a signed quotation
- Approve a quotation on the client's behalf
- Schedule installation
- Advise on site preparation until verified
