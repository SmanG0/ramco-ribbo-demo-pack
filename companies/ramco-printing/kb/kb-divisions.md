# The Four Divisions

> **Source:** ramcoprinting.com/the-company. Retrieved 25 July 2026.
> **This is the most important file in this build.** Every enquiry must be routed to a division before anything else happens, and routing accuracy is the biggest single driver of how good this bot feels.

## The four

Ramco Printing formed four divisions to service growing client needs. Each operates as a **separate unit** but forms a synergy, positioning Ramco Printing as a one-stop shop for all print needs.

| Division | Scope |
|---|---|
| **UNO** | Digital & commercial print |
| **DUO** | Packaging & publishing printing |
| **HEX** | Diaries, journals & notebooks |
| **IX** | Branding, signage & promotional items |

**They are on two different sites.** UNO is at Dunga Close, Industrial Area. DUO, HEX and IX are at Ramco Group Industrial Park, Mombasa Road. See `kb-locations.md` — this affects collection and delivery.

## Overall capability

Off-set, digital, screen and large-format print, plus promotional items. In-house **book binding** since 2013. **ISO 9001:2015** certified.

## Boundary cases — DO NOT GUESS

`[VERIFY]` The following are genuinely ambiguous from published information. **Confirm each with a Ramco Printing estimator before go-live**, then move it into the routing table above.

| Enquiry | Candidates | Why ambiguous |
|---|---|---|
| Catalogues | UNO or DUO | Commercial print, but multi-page and often bound |
| Product labels | DUO or IX | Packaging component, but also branding |
| **Branded notebooks** | HEX or IX | HEX manufactures notebooks, IX does the branding |
| Annual reports | UNO or DUO | Commercial print, but bound and publication-like |
| Presentation folders | UNO or IX | Commercial print item, but often a promotional piece |
| Books needing binding | DUO + binding | Confirm how the binding division sits in the workflow |

**Branded notebooks is the one that matters most** — it is the opening enquiry in the demo script. Settle it first.

Until a case is confirmed, the bot must **ask one clarifying question** rather than guess. For a branded notebook enquiry: "Is this primarily a notebook we'd manufacture for you, or a branding job on stock we source?" Then route on the answer.

## How to route

1. Read the enquiry for the **end product**, not the customer's vocabulary. "Something for the conference" is not a division; "500 notebooks" is.
2. If it maps cleanly to the table, route silently and move on to specification.
3. If it is a boundary case, ask **one** question.
4. If it spans divisions — a full brand rollout needing signage, stationery and packaging — do not split it. Capture it as one project enquiry and hand off to a salesperson. Multi-division projects need a person to coordinate.

Never announce the routing mechanically. "That's one for our HEX division" is fine and reassuring. "I have classified your enquiry as HEX" is not.

## What each division shares

All four take a specification, quote, proof, produce, and dispatch. The proofing stage is common to all of them and is where jobs most often stall — see `kb-proofing.md`.
