## What it is

Safarilink operates a codeshare agreement with Kenya Airways. A codeshare means a flight operated by one airline can be sold and ticketed under the other airline's flight number, so a passenger can book a through itinerary combining Kenya Airways international services with Safarilink domestic and regional services on a single booking.

For a safari traveller this is the practical route from a long-haul arrival into a bush airstrip.

## What to confirm on every codeshare enquiry

**Which airport.** Kenya Airways long-haul operates from JKIA. Safarilink operates from Wilson. A codeshare itinerary will normally require a **surface transfer between the two airports**, which the passenger needs to plan for. Do not assume the connection is airside.

**Baggage.** Baggage allowances differ substantially between the two carriers. Kenya Airways international allowances are considerably more generous than Safarilink's 15 kg or 20 kg. A passenger arriving with a full international allowance will very likely be over the limit for their Safarilink sector.

This is honest and useful regardless of how the through-checking works.

**Connection time.** Given the JKIA to Wilson transfer and Nairobi traffic, connection planning matters. Confirm minimum connection times with reservations rather than estimating.
