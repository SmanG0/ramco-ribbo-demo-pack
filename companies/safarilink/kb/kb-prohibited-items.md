# Prohibited Items

> **Authoritative source:** flysafarilink.com/travel-information/prohibited-items
> `[VERIFY — the full Safarilink prohibited items list must be retrieved from the page above and reproduced here in full before go-live. Do not answer prohibited-items questions from general aviation knowledge.]`

## Why this file must be exact

Prohibited items are a safety and regulatory matter governed by Kenya Civil Aviation Authority requirements and Safarilink's own operating procedures. Getting this wrong in either direction causes harm: telling a passenger they can carry something they cannot means it is confiscated at check-in, and telling them they cannot carry something they can means they leave necessary equipment behind.

**Until the official list is loaded into this document, the bot must not enumerate prohibited items.** The correct response is to direct the passenger to the published list at flysafarilink.com/travel-information/prohibited-items and offer to connect them to reservations for anything specific.

## Structure to populate

When the official list is retrieved, organise it as:

1. **Items prohibited entirely** — not carried in cabin or hold under any circumstances
2. **Items permitted in hold only**
3. **Items permitted in cabin only**
4. **Items requiring advance arrangement** — declared and approved before travel

## Known specifics

**Large sports equipment** — surfboards, bicycles, golf bags and similar are not carried unless requested in advance. Golf bags may be considered on an individual basis on Dash 8 operated routes, charged at USD 20 per bag per sector. Source: flysafarilink.com baggage page.

## Handling these questions

Ask what the specific item is. Then either answer from the verified list, or direct them to the published page and offer a handoff.

For anything touching lithium batteries, medical equipment, oxygen, firearms, ammunition, or hunting or fishing equipment, **always hand off to a colleague** regardless of what the list says. These carry regulatory consequences and a passenger needs a person who can confirm against current requirements and the specific aircraft type.
